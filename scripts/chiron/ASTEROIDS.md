## Addendum (2026-10-24, ~05:30 CEST): Ceres, Pallas, Juno, Vesta on the shared Chebyshev model

Branch `exp/chiron` (worktree /workspace/celestine-chiron), commits b4b0352 and later. Nothing pushed (only the backup hook), no tags, budgets untouched.

**Design.** One shared module, `src/ephemeris/cheb/chebyshev.ts` (793 B gz): a lazy decoder plus a Clenshaw evaluator plus a mean Kepler orbit. It serves all five bodies. Each body's model works like this:
- The base is a 12-parameter mean Kepler orbit, fitted by least squares to Horizons over 1800–2200.
- On top of it sits an adaptive piecewise Chebyshev fit of the residual in spherical coordinates (Δλ·a, Δβ·a, Δr).
- The coefficients are stored as packed zigzag base-32 varints, with each segment's start value delta-coded against the previous segment's end value.

Per-body data is generated by `scripts/chiron/gen-cheb.py` into `src/ephemeris/cheb/<body>-data.ts`. Outside the span (and for NaN) the evaluator returns `undefined`, and the existing Keplerian code stays as the fallback. The public API is unchanged.

**Data.** The fit uses JPL Horizons VECTORS for 1; 2; 3; 4; and 2060;, all over the same span as Chiron:
- 500@10, ecliptic J2000, ICRF, TDB
- 2-day grid, 1795–2205
- fetched sequentially, 41 decade requests per body

For held-out testing I fetched 400 uniform-random off-grid epochs per body, both as VECTORS and as OBSERVER QUANTITIES 31. These are in `scripts/chiron/holdout/`.

**Bug fixed during the work.** Held-out error exceeded the fit tolerance (up to 1.4″ at a 0.8″ target). The cause was that segment endpoints were not constrained by training rows, together with an overfit path for very short segments. The fix:
- Endpoints are always training rows.
- The minimum segment length is 8·(deg+1) days.

After the fix, held-out error is within tolerance everywhere.

### Errors vs Horizons, 400 random epochs per body, 1800–2200

There are two error measures:
- **Apparent:** `getXPosition` against Horizons apparent ecliptic of date. Horizons' nutation is removed because Celestine outputs the mean ecliptic.
- **Model:** the heliocentric J2000 error projected to a geocentric angle.

How to read this:
- The 0.2.1 and main heliocentric (J2000) errors are identical, because they use the same Keplerian elements. The differences between them come from the apparent pipeline.
- Main is worse than 0.2.1 for Ceres, Juno and Vesta (RMS 3.4°→5.1°, 8.3°→9.8°, 42′→68′). It is better for Pallas and Chiron.
- Apparent max slightly above 1″ comes from the shared apparent pipeline and from my nutation approximation in the comparison. The model itself is under 0.85″.

The ≤3″ tier (generated, measured, not committed) has these model max / RMS errors: Chiron 1.96″ / 0.69″, Ceres 2.66″ / 0.98″, Pallas 2.23″ / 0.74″, Juno 2.19″ / 0.74″ and Vesta 2.63″ / 0.95″. Its apparent max is at most 2.85″.

### Things tried that did not pay off

- **Pure Chebyshev without the Kepler base:** about 2× larger.
- **Cartesian residual:** about 5% larger than spherical.
- **Fourier terms in Jupiter/Saturn arguments on top of the base:** about 5% smaller, but not worth the extra code.
- **Higher degree:** helps the ≤3″ tier and Chiron, and hurts Ceres and Vesta at ≤1″.
- **Cross-body gzip sharing:** happens automatically in the bundle, and gains are small.

Main-belt bodies come within about 1 AU of Earth, so 1″ ≈ 5e-6 AU. Every body costs 4–8 KB because of that, while Chiron (≥7 AU away) costs about 1.8 KB.

### Decision levers for Fox

- **Accuracy:** with these models every body is about 10⁴× more accurate than both 0.2.1 and main.
- **Ways to avoid the ~22–28 KB in the root bundle:**
  - (a) Put the asteroid data behind a subpath or lazy import, keeping Keplerian in the root.
  - (b) Use the ≤3″ tier.
  - (c) Shorten the span, for example 1900–2100 at roughly half the size. Not measured, so treat that as an estimate.
- **Cheapest way to make no body less accurate than 0.2.1:** the apparent-pipeline regression on Ceres, Juno and Vesta is a separate issue from the Keplerian elements, and is worth bisecting.
